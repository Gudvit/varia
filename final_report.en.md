## 1. How to read this document

This report was compiled by the cat’s owner from photographs of paper and electronic medical records (visit records, laboratory results, histopathology and cytology reports, in-house lab printouts, radiographs) obtained from several veterinary clinics and laboratories. The documents were uploaded out of order and in batches; this report is the result of combining them into a single chronology checked for contradictions.

**This document does not replace examination and assessment by a veterinarian.** Its purpose is to give a new vet, oncologist or any consulting specialist the most complete and accurate picture of the medical history, without spending time sorting through dozens of scattered photographs.

#### Principles

- **Facts from documents** are given with the date and source; when the original (usually in Polish) is quoted directly, a translation is provided.
- **The owner’s words** (history, observations at home) are marked separately as “per the owner” — these are not objective clinical data.
- **Gaps in the data** are marked as “not stated”, “document not uploaded”, “needs confirmation” — where information is missing, nothing is assumed.
- The **⚠️** sign marks discrepancies and contradictions between documents — both versions are given without trying to decide which is correct; the full list is in section 15.
- Laboratory values are given with units and the reference range of the laboratory that ran the test (the LoVet in-house lab uses different ranges from IDEXX — comparing numbers directly between the two is not valid).
- Lay (non-veterinary) descriptions of images — for example, radiographs without an attached report — are marked separately and must not be taken as a diagnosis.
